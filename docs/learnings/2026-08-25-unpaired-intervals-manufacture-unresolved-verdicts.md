fact: A question recorded as "not resolved at this sample size" may be resolved already, with no new data, if the verdict was reached using an UNPAIRED interval where the conditions actually share their evaluation set. This repo had recorded mapped-k1 vs mapped-k4 as unresolved at n=500 on the grounds that their Wilson intervals overlap ([0.504, 0.591] vs [0.454, 0.542]). Wilson intervals are unpaired and cannot use the fact that both conditions score the SAME 500 examples. An exact McNemar test on the per-example correctness vectors resolves it: 41 examples flipped one way against 16 the other, p = 1.26e-03, which survives Bonferroni for the family of six comparisons run (threshold 8.3e-03). Consequence for this repository specifically: the monotone accuracy decline across k = 1, 4, 8 now holds on EVERY adjacent pair rather than resting on the k=1 vs k=8 endpoints, which strengthens its central claim that selecting k on in-sample R^2 picks the worst option. The reciprocal discipline matters as much: the same pass gave mapped-k8 vs identity p = 4.74e-02, below 0.05 but ABOVE the Bonferroni threshold, and that one was left recorded as open rather than upgraded. Before accepting any "intervals overlap, therefore unresolved" verdict, check whether the conditions are paired; if they are, an unpaired interval is the wrong instrument and the verdict is an artifact of the instrument rather than of the data. Note the failure was not only statistical: `kvt/stats.py` had been built and then had no caller anywhere outside its own tests, which is why the paired test was never applied to data that had been sitting on disk since the previous build.
